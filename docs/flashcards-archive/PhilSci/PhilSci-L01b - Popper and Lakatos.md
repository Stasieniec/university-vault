> [!exam]- Mock exam Q2 (10 points): Imre Lakatos analyses the development of science in terms of "scientific research programmes". Describe the components of research programmes, and explain what, according to Lakatos, scientific progress consists in.
> Follow the official key: "(1) Hard core; (2) protective belt with auxiliary hypotheses; (3) positive heuristic; (4) negative heuristic. Progressive programme: leads to new discoveries. Degenerating programme: only accommodates known facts."
> 1. **Unit of appraisal:** a research programme, a series of theories over time sharing a common structure, rather than an isolated hypothesis.
> 2. **Hard core:** the fundamental assumptions that give the programme its identity (Newton: three laws of mechanics plus the law of gravitation), held irrefutable by **methodological decision**.
> 3. **Protective belt:** auxiliary hypotheses, initial conditions, theories of the instruments. It meets the evidence and is what gets modified when an anomaly appears.
> 4. **Positive heuristic:** the problem-solving machinery, a partially articulated plan for developing the belt (which models to build, which anomalies to digest). Example: the Newtonian checks atmospheric refraction, light in magnetic storms, may invent an unknown planet and calculate its position, mass and velocity.
> 5. **Negative heuristic:** never direct modus tollens at the hard core; absorb anomalies in the belt.
> 6. **Progress:** cannot be "unrefuted", since all programmes grow in an ocean of anomalies. **Progressive** = predicts novel facts (Halley's comet returning after seventy-two years; Einstein's eclipse star separations). **Degenerating** = theories fabricated only to accommodate known facts, lagging behind them (Marxism, theory of imperialism cooked up after the event).
> 7. Revolutions: scientists desert a degenerating programme for a progressive rival.
>
> Losing marks: calling the hard core unfalsifiable in principle (it is protected by decision), or treating every belt modification as ad hoc.

> [!exam]- Mock exam Q3 (10 points): Popper praised Albert Einstein's theory of general relativity as a truly scientific theory, citing the example of Eddington's expedition. This expedition set out to measure the shift of a distant star during a solar eclipse. Einstein's theory states that large masses, like the sun, curve space-time. Thus, incoming light rays will follow a curved path around the sun and will appear in a different spot as seen from the earth. The theory makes a very precise prediction about this shift. In 1953, however, Einstein was asked how he would have responded to a measurement outcome that did not show this shift: "Then I am very sorry for the dear Lord [Eddington], for the theory is correct." Would, and-or could, this be acceptable for Popper? Explain your answer.
> Follow the official key:
> 1. **No**, assuming the measurement was correct: the theory is falsified and has to be rejected. Einstein shows a **dogmatic attitude**. Eddington's test is exactly the risky prediction that makes relativity scientific: it forbids the null result everyone before Einstein expected.
> 2. Einstein's move is the Marxists' move: re-interpreting to escape refutation, which rescues the theory only at the price of destroying or lowering its scientific status.
> 3. **However**, perhaps the measurement was not correct. The prediction comes from the theory plus auxiliary assumptions (telescope optics, photographic plates, data reduction, star identification). Given the **Duhem-Quine thesis**, scientists can always put the blame elsewhere and retain the theory.
> 4. For Popper this is acceptable **only if there are independent reasons for rejecting the measurement result**, reasons other than that the theory would otherwise be refuted. Without them it is an **ad hoc** hypothesis (a conventionalist stratagem).
> 5. Einstein as quoted gives no independent reason, only confidence in the theory, so the reply is not acceptable.
>
> Losing marks: stopping at "no"; letting Duhem-Quine excuse Einstein without the independent-reasons condition; saying Popper thinks a falsified theory is proved false and gone forever.

> [!exam]- State Popper's criterion of demarcation and use it to classify Einstein's relativity, astrology, Marx's theory of history and psychoanalysis. Why do the last two fail in different ways?
> - **Criterion:** "the criterion of the scientific status of a theory is its falsifiability, or refutability, or testability." A theory must be capable of conflicting with possible, conceivable observations.
> - **Einstein:** scientific. The 1919 eclipse prediction was risky: had the shift been absent the theory would simply have been refuted.
> - **Astrology:** fails. Astrologers were impressed by confirmations and unimpressed by unfavourable evidence, and made prophecies vague enough to explain anything: "in order to escape falsification they destroyed the testability of their theory".
> - **Marxism:** fails, but **not originally**. Early versions (the coming social revolution) were testable and **in fact falsified**; followers re-interpreted theory and evidence to agree, a conventionalist twist that made it irrefutable.
> - **Psychoanalysis (Freud, Adler):** simply non-testable from the start. No conceivable human behaviour could contradict it (the two men and the child).
> - So Marxism was once science and was destroyed by its defenders; psychoanalysis never made a risky claim.

> [!exam]- What is Lakatos's main objection to Popper's falsificationism, and how does the methodology of scientific research programmes answer it without following Kuhn?
> - **Objection:** Popper's criterion "ignores the remarkable tenacity of scientific theories. Scientists have thick skins." Faced with contrary facts they invent a rescue hypothesis for a "mere anomaly", or ignore it and work on other problems.
> - Crucial-experiment stories are "fabricated long after the theory had been abandoned". A Newtonian asked what would make him abandon Newton "would have been exactly as nonplussed as are some Marxists", so Popper's test fails to separate Newton from Marx.
> - All theories are born refuted (lunar motion and Newton; Kaufmann and relativity), so "unrefuted" cannot mark science.
> - **Not Kuhn:** Kuhn concluded theory change is irrational; then there is no demarcation, no distinction between progress and decay, no objective standard of honesty.
> - **Answer:** shift the unit from the theory to the research programme (hard core, belt, heuristics) and the test from refutation to **novel prediction**: progressive programmes predict novel facts, degenerating ones accommodate known facts.
> - Revolutions are rational: scientists join the progressive programme, with no instant rationality.

> [!exam]- Popper and Lakatos both discuss modifying auxiliary hypotheses to save a theory from contrary evidence. Compare how each judges such a modification, using an example.
> - **Popper:** the modification is judged by its **motive and form**. If introduced only to absorb the refutation (ad hoc: for this purpose only, adding no new testable consequences) it is a conventionalist stratagem that lowers scientific status. Blaming the measurement is allowed only with **independent reasons**.
> - **Lakatos:** belt modification is **normal, constant and necessary**, since that is what the positive heuristic does. It is judged only by its **consequences**: did the modified programme go on to predict a novel fact that was found?
> - Example: an invented planet to explain an orbital anomaly and the Marxists' theory of imperialism are the same manoeuvre in Popper's terms. In Lakatos's terms one can lead to a novel discovery and the other produced nothing. (Background example, which Lakatos does not name: the planet postulated to explain Uranus's orbit was found as Neptune, while the same move for Mercury gave Vulcan, never found.)
>
> Losing marks: treating every protective-belt modification as automatically ad hoc.

> [!exam]- Contrast Carnap's verifiability criterion with Popper's falsifiability criterion: what each demarcates, its verdict on metaphysics, and the direction of its logic.
> - **Verifiability (Carnap, Vienna Circle):** a statement is meaningful if its truth can in principle be determined by observation or experiment; "the meaning of a statement is its method of verification". Demarcates **meaningful from meaningless**. Metaphysics is nonsense, pseudo-statements to be eliminated.
> - **Falsifiability (Popper):** a statement is scientific if capable of conflicting with possible observations. Demarcates **science from non-science**. Non-scientific statements are meaningful, sometimes important, sometimes the ancestors of science (Empedocles, Parmenides).
> - **Logic:** verification wants observations to establish universal laws, which is impossible; falsification wants observations able to destroy them, which modus tollens permits.
> - Popper: his problem "was neither a problem of meaningfulness or significance, nor a problem of truth or acceptability" but of separating the empirical sciences from all other statements.

> [!exam]- Compare Popper, Kuhn and Lakatos on the unit of appraisal, the demarcation criterion, and how theory change happens.
> - **Unit:** Popper, the individual theory; Kuhn, the paradigm and its community; Lakatos, the research programme (a series of theories over time).
> - **Demarcation:** Popper, falsifiability (a potential falsifier specified in advance); Kuhn, no explicit criterion (puzzle-solving tradition, stable community); Lakatos, progressive versus degenerating (novel predictions).
> - **Contrary evidence:** Popper, refutes the theory, which must be rejected; Kuhn, an anomaly tolerated until anomalies pile up into crisis; Lakatos, hits the belt and is digested by the positive heuristic.
> - **Tenacity:** Popper, a vice; Kuhn, functional in normal science; Lakatos, rational and often correct.
> - **Theory change:** Popper, a crucial experiment kills the theory (rational by logic); Kuhn, a revolution or Gestalt switch, no algorithm (charged with irrationalism); Lakatos, desertion of a degenerating programme for a progressive rival, rational but no instant rationality.
> - **Crucial experiments:** Popper, the heart of method; Kuhn, rare and reconstructed after the fact; Lakatos, a myth.

> [!card]- What is a demarcation criterion, and why is checking where work is published and funded not an answer to the problem of demarcation?
> A **necessary condition** any discipline must satisfy to count as genuine science; failing it makes the discipline non-scientific. Finding one is the **problem of demarcation**. Checking publication venues (popular magazines versus refereed journals), funding or academy membership only describes how the community labels a field. That is sociology; philosophers want the **reasons** that would make an accusation of pseudoscience correct.

> [!card]- What was John A. Wheeler's 1979 complaint to the AAAS, and what does it illustrate?
> In 1969 the AAAS admitted the Parapsychological Association (ESP, telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, telekinesis) as an affiliate. In 1979 Wheeler demanded its expulsion: "We have enough charlatanism in this country today without needing a scientific organization to prostitute itself to it." It is an accusation of **pseudoscience**, and the philosophical question is what would make it correct. (The association is still a member.)

> [!card]- Curd and Cover set four answers to the demarcation problem against each other. Whose, and what do the three non-Popperians share? Who debated creation-science?
> Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos and Thagard. Popper's falsifiability is the most influential, especially among scientists. Kuhn, Lakatos and Thagard all reject falsifiability as the hallmark of science and disagree on the replacement, but all make scientific legitimacy depend on **historical** considerations, how theories develop over time. **Michael Ruse**, expert witness in the Arkansas creationism trial, guided the judge to five criteria (including falsifiability) that creation-science failed; **Larry Laudan** attacks the list and doubts any criterion all sciences satisfy.

> [!card]- Which question did Popper set himself in 1919, which two questions did he set aside, and why can truth not be the criterion?
> "When should a theory be ranked as **scientific**?" He set aside when a theory is **true** and when it is **acceptable**. "I wished to distinguish between science and pseudo-science; knowing very well that **science often errs**, and that **pseudo-science may happen to stumble on the truth**." Scientific theories are often wrong and fortune-tellers can get lucky, so truth and scientific status come apart.

> [!card]- What was the standard answer to demarcation that Popper rejected, and why does astrology show it fails?
> Science is marked by its **empirical, inductive method**, proceeding from observation and experiment. Popper reframed the problem as separating a genuinely empirical method from a **pseudo-empirical** one, which appeals to observation but falls short of scientific standards. Astrology has a "stupendous mass of empirical evidence based on observation, on horoscopes and on biographies", so supporting evidence alone cannot be what makes science different.

> [!card]- Which four theories occupied Popper in Vienna in 1919, what was his eventual verdict on each, and what did NOT bother him about three of them?
> - Einstein's relativity: science.
> - Marx's theory of history: testable once, then rescued into pseudoscience.
> - Freud's psychoanalysis and Adler's individual psychology: simply non-testable.
>
> What bothered him was not their truth (few believed Einstein's theory true either) or that physics is more exact or measurable. It was that the three, "though posing as sciences", resembled myths, "astrology rather than astronomy".

> [!card]- Why did Popper come to see the apparent explanatory power of Marxism, Freud and Adler as a weakness? Give the three vignettes.
> Admirers saw confirmations everywhere and treated unbelievers as blinded by class interest or repression. Popper: "this apparent strength was in fact their weakness."
> - **Marxist:** confirmation on every page of a newspaper, in the news, its presentation, and what it did not say.
> - **Freud:** analysts' theories constantly "verified" by clinical observations.
> - **Adler:** analysed a case Popper brought him without seeing the child, "Because of my thousandfold experience." Popper: "And with this new case, I suppose, your experience has become thousand-and-one-fold."
>
> The thousand cases only confirm that a case **can be** interpreted by the theory, which is worthless when every case can be.

> [!card]- Give Popper's two-men-and-a-child example: how do Freud and Adler explain each case, and what is the point?
> - **Case A**, a man pushes a child into water to drown it. Freud: repression (some component of his Oedipus complex). Adler: inferiority feelings, needing to prove he dared commit a crime.
> - **Case B**, a man dies trying to save the child. Freud: sublimation. Adler: inferiority feelings, needing to prove he dared rescue the child.
>
> Opposite behaviours, confident explanations, no risk. Popper "could not think of any human behaviour which could not be interpreted in terms of either theory."

> [!card]- Describe Eddington's 1919 eclipse test of Einstein's theory: the prediction, the method, and why Popper found it impressive. What did Popper's footnote add?
> - **Prediction:** light is attracted by heavy bodies, so a star whose apparent position is near the sun appears **shifted away from the sun** by a precise angle.
> - **Method:** photograph the stars during a total eclipse, photograph the same constellation at night, compare the star separations. Two Royal Astronomical Society expeditions found the predicted deflection.
> - **Why impressive:** the **risk**. The theory was incompatible with results everyone before Einstein would have expected; a definitely absent effect would simply refute it.
> - **Footnote:** a slight oversimplification, since about half the effect follows from classical theory with a ballistic theory of light. The full deflection is twice the Newtonian value, so the test discriminates by a factor of two.

> [!card]- State Popper's seven conclusions (winter 1919-20) from *Conjectures and Refutations*.
> 1. It is easy to obtain confirmations for nearly every theory, if we look for them.
> 2. Confirmations count only if they result from **risky predictions**: unenlightened by the theory, we would have expected an event incompatible with it.
> 3. Every good scientific theory is a **prohibition**: the more it forbids, the better it is.
> 4. A theory not refutable by any conceivable event is non-scientific. **Irrefutability is not a virtue of a theory but a vice.**
> 5. Every genuine test is an attempt to falsify. **Testability is falsifiability**, and it comes in **degrees**: some theories take greater risks.
> 6. Confirming evidence counts only from a genuine test, a serious but unsuccessful attempt to falsify: **corroborating evidence**.
> 7. Testable theories found false can be upheld by introducing an **ad hoc** auxiliary assumption or re-interpreting the theory ad hoc. Always possible, but it destroys or lowers scientific status: a **conventionalist twist** or **stratagem**.

> [!card]- Explain Popper's claim that "the more a theory forbids, the better it is". What is the limiting case, and what is the astrologer's trick?
> A bold, high-content theory forbids most conceivable observation reports, so it has many ways to be wrong: risky and highly testable. A vague theory forbids almost nothing: safe and barely testable. Limiting case: a theory that forbids nothing is compatible with every possible observation, irrefutable and non-scientific. The astrologer's trick is to make prophecies vague enough to move the theory from the first kind to the second.

> [!card]- State the logical asymmetry between verification and falsification of a universal law, with the formal steps. What is the mirror case?
> Law: $\forall x\,(Sx \rightarrow Wx)$, "all swans are white", ranging over all swans including unobserved ones.
> - **Verification fails:** no finite conjunction $(Sa_1 \wedge Wa_1) \wedge \dots \wedge (Sa_n \wedge Wa_n)$ deductively yields the universal statement (Hume's problem of induction). Lakatos: "there can be no valid derivation of a law of nature from any finite number of facts".
> - **Falsification succeeds:** $Sa \wedge \neg Wa$ entails $\neg\forall x\,(Sx \rightarrow Wx)$. General form, modus tollens:
>
> $$\frac{T \rightarrow O, \quad \neg O}{\therefore \neg T}$$
>
> Mirror case: existential statements ("there exists a white raven") are provable by one sighting and unfalsifiable by any number of failures to sight one.

> [!card]- What does "corroborated" mean for Popper, and why is "well corroborated, therefore probably true" a mistake?
> Corroborated means the theory has survived our best attempts to refute it so far: a report on its past track record. It is not a probability of truth. Popper argued (1934) that the mathematical probability of any theory, scientific or not, given any amount of evidence, is **zero**. Inferring probable truth from corroboration is exactly the inductive step Popper denies. Science makes bold **conjectures** and tries to destroy them; it never proves.

> [!card]- Does Popper think Freud and Adler see nothing correctly? What exactly is his verdict about?
> No. Much of what they say may be important and "may well play its part one day in a psychological science which is testable." But clinical observations cannot confirm them any more than astrologers' daily confirmations. They "describe some facts, but in the manner of myths", with interesting suggestions "but not in a testable form". The verdict is about the **form** of the claims. Freud's epic of Ego, Super-ego and Id has no stronger claim to scientific status than Homer's stories from Olympus.

> [!card]- What is Popper's point about clinical observations, and what is the Oedipus effect?
> Clinical observations, like all observations, are **interpretations in the light of theories**, so they seem to support the theory used to interpret them. Real support needs tests, for which **criteria of refutation must be laid down beforehand**. Analysts never agreed which responses would refute psychoanalysis itself, and concepts like "ambivalence" make that nearly impossible. The **Oedipus effect** (Popper's term) is the influence of a theory, expectation or prediction on the event it predicts, as the oracle's prophecy started the chain leading to Oedipus's parricide; analysts' expectations may shape patients' responses (Freud admits most dreams used in analysis owe their origin to the analyst's suggestion). Popper's Trollope epigraph, a prophet working to verify his own prophecy, jokes at the same mechanism.

> [!card]- Why does Popper say astrology was attacked for the wrong reason, and what follows about the origin of an idea?
> Aristotelians and rationalists down to Newton attacked astrology for asserting that planets "influence" terrestrial events, which is now accepted. Newton's gravity, and especially the lunar theory of the tides, was historically an offspring of astrological lore; Newton adopted it reluctantly, and Galileo rejected the lunar theory of tides, probably for the same reason. So the pseudoscientific origin of an idea does not settle its scientific status.

> [!card]- Define an ad hoc rescue and the conventionalist stratagem in Popper's sense. What does "ad hoc" mean, and is the manoeuvre ever available?
> When a testable theory is contradicted, defenders introduce an auxiliary assumption, or re-interpret the theory, solely so that it escapes refutation. **Ad hoc** means "for this purpose only": the modification is motivated only by absorbing the refuting evidence and adds no new testable consequences. The manoeuvre is **always available**; the cost is that it rescues the theory "only at the price of destroying, or at least lowering, its scientific status." Popper's names: **conventionalist twist** or **conventionalist stratagem**.

> [!card]- Under what condition may a Popperian blame an auxiliary assumption or the measurement instead of the theory?
> A prediction is never derived from the theory alone, so blame can in principle go elsewhere (the Duhem-Quine point). Popper allows it **only if there are independent reasons for rejecting the measurement**, reasons not consisting merely in the fact that the theory would otherwise be refuted. With independent reasons it is a normal correction; without them it is an ad hoc hypothesis and the theory's status drops. Falsification is therefore a decision to reject taken under ambiguity about where the blame lies, rather than a proof that the theory is false forever.

> [!card]- Why does Popper deny that non-scientific means meaningless? Give his examples.
> Myths can develop and become testable; nearly all scientific theories originate from myths, and myths can anticipate science. **Empedocles'** evolution by trial and error; **Parmenides'** unchanging block universe, which with an added dimension becomes Einstein's block universe. A non-scientific theory "is not thereby found to be unimportant, or insignificant, or meaningless, or nonsensical"; it just cannot claim scientific empirical support. Popper also names the racialist interpretation of history as another all-explanatory pseudo-scientific theory as influential as Marxism.

> [!card]- Why does Lakatos say demarcation is not armchair philosophy? Give his three historical cases.
> It has "grave ethical and political implications" and implications "for the institutionalization of criticism".
> - The Catholic Church banned Copernicus's theory as pseudoscientific in **1616** and took it off the Index in **1820**, deeming the facts had proved it.
> - The Soviet Central Committee declared Mendelian genetics pseudoscientific in **1949**; advocates such as Vavilov were killed in camps. Genetics was rehabilitated but the Party kept the right to decide.
> - The Western liberal Establishment denies free speech to what it regards as pseudoscience, as in the race and intelligence debate.

> [!card]- Why is knowledge not strong belief, according to Lakatos? What is his example and his principle?
> Many people have been totally committed to absurd beliefs (demons, angels, heaven and hell). Scientists are the ones who do not believe their theories very hard: **Newton never believed bodies attract at a distance**. "Blind commitment to a theory is not an intellectual virtue: it is an intellectual crime." A statement may be pseudoscientific though everyone believes it, and valuable though nobody believes or understands it. Principle: a theory's objective value is independent of the mind that creates or understands it and depends only on its objective support in facts.

> [!card]- What is Hume's fork, and why does Lakatos say witchcraft shows we cannot apply it straightforwardly?
> Hume (end of the *Enquiry*): a volume with no abstract reasoning about quantity or number and no experimental reasoning about matter of fact should be committed to the flames, as "sophistry and illusion". But seventeenth-century witchcraft literature is full of careful observations, sworn evidence and experiments, and **Glanvill**, house philosopher of the early Royal Society, took witchcraft as the paradigm of experimental reasoning. "We have to define experimental reasoning before we start Humean book burning."

> [!card]- Why does Lakatos say Newton's claim to have deduced his laws from Kepler's phenomena was false?
> Kepler: planets move in ellipses. Newton's theory: they would do so **only if** they did not disturb each other, but they do, so his perturbation theory implies **no planet moves in an ellipse**. The premises Newton claimed to deduce from are inconsistent with what he deduced: he replaced Kepler's laws instead of deriving his from them. Generally, no law of nature can be validly derived from finitely many facts.

> [!card]- How does Lakatos explain why scientists kept demanding that theories be proven from facts? Give the Ampère example.
> Science was born in the seventeenth century, when the key knowledge was theological and had to be beyond doubt. The Enlightenment denied theological knowledge but judged the new knowledge of Nature by **standards taken over from theology**: it had to be proven, and unproven theories were heresy. Ampère titled his book *Mathematical Theory of Electrodynamic Phenomena Unequivocally Deduced from Experiment*, then admitted some experiments were never performed and the instruments never built. Only the fall of Newtonian theory showed these standards were utopian.

> [!card]- What is probabilism as a demarcation criterion, what is its attraction, and why does it collapse?
> Twentieth-century **inductive logicians** define the probability of theories relative to the total evidence: high probability means scientific, and honesty means saying only what is highly probable. Attraction: a **continuous scale** instead of a black-and-white line. Collapse: Popper argued in **1934** that the probability of every theory, given any evidence, is **zero**, so all theories are equally improbable and the scale shrinks to a point.

> [!card]- How does Lakatos formulate Popper's criterion, and what three consequences does he draw from it?
> A theory is scientific if one is prepared to specify **in advance** a crucial experiment that could falsify it, and pseudoscientific if one refuses to specify such a **potential falsifier**.
> 1. A theory can be scientific with no supporting evidence and pseudoscientific with all the evidence: scientific status is fixed **independently of the facts**.
> 2. It demarcates **scientific method from non-scientific method** rather than theories: the same proposition can be science or dogma depending on its holders' attitude.
> 3. The test question: ask a Marxist "what conceivable event would make him abandon his Marxism"; if committed, he finds it immoral to specify one.

> [!card]- What do scientists actually do when facts contradict their theory, according to Lakatos, and what does that do to Popper's criterion?
> "Scientists have thick skins." They (1) invent a rescue hypothesis to explain what they call a **mere anomaly**, or (2) if they cannot, **ignore** it and turn to other problems. They speak of anomalies and recalcitrant instances, not refutations, and crucial-experiment stories are "fabricated long after the theory had been abandoned". Some Newtonians asked to name a falsifier would be "exactly as nonplussed as are some Marxists", so the criterion fails to demarcate.

> [!card]- How does Lakatos describe Kuhn's response to the failure of naive falsificationism, and why does he refuse it?
> Kuhn, "a distinguished American philosopher of science", concluded from the naivety of Popper's falsificationism that theory change is a conversion and revolutions are irrational. Lakatos refuses because then there is no explicit demarcation, no distinction between scientific progress and intellectual decay, and no objective standard of honesty. He wants a third option keeping rationality without pretending scientists behave like Popperians.

> [!card]- What is Lakatos's unit of appraisal, and why can a single hypothesis not be called progressive or degenerating?
> "The typical descriptive unit of great scientific achievements is not an isolated hypothesis but rather a **research programme**": a series of theories over time with a common structure and plan. Progressive and degenerating describe a **direction of travel**, and a single snapshot has none. Trivial trial and error, like refuting "all swans are white" with a black swan, "does not rank as science".

> [!card]- Lakatos's hard core and negative heuristic: what are they, what is the Newtonian example, and why is the core irrefutable?
> - **Hard core:** the fundamental assumptions giving the programme its identity. Newton: the three laws of mechanics plus the law of gravitation. Abandoning it means leaving the programme.
> - **Negative heuristic:** never direct modus tollens at the hard core; refuting instances must be absorbed in the belt.
> - The core is irrefutable **by methodological decision** of practitioners, not by logic or meaning. That is why the protection is revocable when the programme degenerates.

> [!card]- Lakatos's protective belt and positive heuristic: what does each contain or do?
> - **Protective belt:** the vast belt of auxiliary hypotheses that "tenaciously" protects the core: initial conditions, assumptions about the apparatus, theories of the instruments, models of the system. It meets the evidence and is modified, replaced and rebuilt when anomalies appear.
> - **Positive heuristic:** "a powerful problem-solving machinery, which, with the help of sophisticated mathematical techniques, digests anomalies and even turns them into positive evidence". A partially articulated plan: which models to build next, which anomalies to attack in which order, which techniques to develop. It lets scientists work for years without being derailed by each recalcitrant fact.
>
> Terminology: Lakatos's Open University broadcast names hard core and belt and speaks of "a heuristic"; the positive and negative split comes from his fuller statement of the methodology, and the mock exam key lists both.

> [!card]- How does a Newtonian astronomer handle a planet that does not move as predicted, according to Lakatos?
> He does not conclude gravitation is false. He checks his conjectures about atmospheric refraction, the propagation of light in magnetic storms, and hundreds of other conjectures in the programme, and "may even invent a hitherto unknown planet and calculate its position, mass and velocity". Every move changes the **protective belt**; the three laws and the inverse square law are never on trial.

> [!card]- What does Lakatos mean by "all theories are born refuted and die refuted"? Give his two examples and the editors' correction.
> Newton, Einstein, quantum mechanics, Marxism and Freudianism are all programmes with unsolved problems at every stage, so science cannot be "still unrefuted". **Lunar motion** refuted Newton when the *Principia* appeared; **Kaufmann** refuted relativity the year it was published. Curd and Cover: Lakatos uses "refuted" loosely (any apparently well-founded inconsistent result), and both refutations were **spurious**: the moon's motion is consistent with Newton, Kaufmann's beta-ray results were experimental error. Had Einstein followed Popper he would have dropped a correct theory over a wrong measurement.

> [!card]- State Lakatos's criterion: what makes a research programme progressive or degenerating, and what kind of question does it make demarcation?
> - **Progressive:** the programme predicts **novel facts**, "undreamt of, or have indeed been contradicted by previous or rival programmes". Theory runs ahead of the facts.
> - **Degenerating:** "theories are fabricated only in order to accommodate known facts". Theory lags behind and runs to catch up.
>
> It demarcates directions of travel over time, so scientific status is a **historical** question about track record, and it can change: a degenerating programme can recover, a progressive one can run out.

> [!card]- Describe the Halley's comet case and why Lakatos treats it as a flagship of a progressive programme.
> In 1686 the current comet theories were that a comet is a warning signal from an angry God, and Kepler's little-known view that comets move in straight lines. Newtonian theory says some comets move in hyperbolas or parabolas and never return, others in ellipses. Halley observed a brief stretch of one comet's path and calculated it would return in **seventy-two years**, to the minute, at a well-defined point of the sky. Long after Newton and Halley died, it returned as predicted: a stunning novel fact.

> [!card]- Besides Halley, what other examples does Lakatos give of progressive programmes, and what does he say about Marxism?
> - Newtonians predicted the existence and exact motion of small **planets never observed** before.
> - Einstein's programme predicted that the distance between two stars measured at night and during a solar eclipse would **differ**, an observation nobody had thought to make. This is Popper's Eddington episode redescribed as novel prediction.
> - Marxism: "Has, for instance, Marxism ever predicted a stunning novel fact successfully? **Never!**"

> [!card]- List the four failed Marxist predictions Lakatos cites and the after-the-event accommodations.
> **Failed:** (1) absolute impoverishment of the working class; (2) the first socialist revolution in the most industrially developed society; (3) socialist societies free of revolutions; (4) no conflict of interests between socialist countries.
> **Accommodations:** a **theory of imperialism** for rising working-class living standards; an explanation of revolution in backward Russia; "explanations" of Berlin 1953, Budapest 1956, Prague 1968 and the Russian-Chinese conflict. All "cooked up after the event to protect Marxian theory from the facts."

> [!card]- Summarise Lakatos's verdict (p. 25) on what counts as the hallmark of empirical progress and failure.
> Not trivial verifications: Popper is right there are millions, and stones falling to earth are no success for Newton. Not "refutations" either, as Popper preached, since "all programmes grow in a permanent ocean of anomalies". What counts are "dramatic, unexpected, stunning predictions": a few tilt the balance. Where theory lags behind the facts, the programme is degenerating.

> [!card]- How do scientific revolutions happen for Lakatos, and what four qualifications does he add (and against whom)?
> With two rivals, one progressing and one degenerating, scientists tend to join the progressive one: "the rationale of scientific revolutions".
> 1. **Against a naive reading of himself:** it is not dishonest to stick to a degenerating programme and try to revive it, but the record must be public.
> 2. **Against Popper:** no **instant rationality**; budding programmes need leniency, and may take decades to become progressive.
> 3. **Against Popper:** "Criticism is not a Popperian quick kill"; "there is **no refutation without a better theory**."
> 4. **Against Kuhn:** revolutions are not sudden, irrational changes in vision.
>
> "The history of science refutes both Popper and Kuhn": crucial experiments and Kuhnian revolutions are both **myths**.

> [!card]- What does Lakatos keep from Popper and from Kuhn, and what is the cost of his position?
> He keeps Popper's demand for objective standards and Kuhn's respect for actual practice, by moving the unit from theory to programme and the test from refutation to novel prediction. Popper stays right that confirmations are cheap, that what matters is what a theory forbids, and that the logic of universal statements is asymmetric. The cost: verdicts are only **retrospective**. You can say a programme has degenerated for thirty years, but cannot tell a scientist today that he must abandon it.
